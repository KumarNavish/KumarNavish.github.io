Trajectory

A trajectory, not a chronology.

The work changes domains, but the reasoning is consistent: expose structure, control adaptation, build inspectable systems, then expand the interface through which people use them.

A useful agent should not merely produce a plausible answer. It should expose exactly which evidence supports each bounded assertion—and preserve uncertainty when evidence is insufficient.

Problem

Fluent systems can turn missing evidence into confident process advice.

Document-heavy workflows need extraction, synthesis, and procedural guidance, but the cost of a plausible unsupported inference can be much higher than the cost of admitting uncertainty.

  • Source artifacts can disagree, omit essential facts, or expose only partial evidence.
  • A free-form response makes observation and interpretation difficult to separate.
  • A trustworthy product needs useful failure states rather than silent optimism.
My role

Scientific framing, product architecture, schema design, evaluation design, frontend, backend, release governance, and end-to-end implementation.

Experience Replay Through the Lens of Optimization

Question
What update is experience replay actually trying to recover?
Contribution
Recasts replay as correction toward the update joint training would have taken, derives a discrete replay selection objective, and introduces Greedy Ball Replay.
Evidence boundary
Mechanism studies and end-to-end experiments connect smaller replay error to better continual-learning outcomes while exposing when the approximation is weak.
Still unresolved
How far can the correction view scale when representations, optimizers, and data distributions all move at once?

Generative AI × spatial computing

The interface should become part of the intelligence.

Today’s AI interfaces compress intelligence into chat. I am exploring systems where language generates spatial worlds, tools, simulations, and agents that can be inhabited, manipulated, and tested.

The world becomes editable.

Generated scenes need spatial semantics, physics, persistence, and direct manipulation so people can revise them through action.

  • How do we evaluate a generated environment as a useful interface rather than an impressive scene?
  • How should a person inspect and revise the hidden state behind spatial generation?
  • What must remain deterministic when the world, tools, and agents are model-produced?
